      How whales are graded

      Every whale carries a grade from A+ to F. It is this site's own automated score — not a rating by Nansen, and not a judgement about any person or firm.

      A+EliteScore 85–100. Strong realised profit across a broad set of coins.
      AStrong handsScore 75–84. Consistently profitable with a real sample behind it.
      BSolidScore 62–74. Good, but thinner evidence — or a narrow range of coins.
      CCoin flipScore 48–61. Roughly break-even. Their side tells you little.
      DShakyScore 35–47. Losing more than winning over the window.
      FFade materialScore under 35. The record argues against following them.

      What goes into it: realised return, win rate and how many trades are behind them over the last 30 days, adjusted by the last 7. A big sample counts for more than a lucky streak, and a whale whose record rests on only one or two coins is capped at B — a win rate built on two coins is easy to inflate. The one exception is a specialist: a whale who trades few coins but has proven realised profit on the exact coin they are opening now.

      A grade describes the past. It is not a prediction, not advice, and an A+ whale is still wrong often — the odds on each card say how often.
